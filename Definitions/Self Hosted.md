# Self-Hosted

## Definition

Self-hosting refers to running and maintaining software services or applications under one’s own control—over infrastructure that is owned, managed, or directly configured by the user or organization—rather than relying on third-party or vendor-hosted services.
## Description

- It gives the user or organization full responsibility over hardware, software stack, updates, security, and data governance.  
- Motivations often include greater autonomy, privacy, customization, and avoiding lock-in or dependency on external providers.  
- Self-hosting often comes with operational overhead: requires technical expertise, resource investment (time, servers), and exposing services securely to the internet.  
- In *To Cloud or not to Cloud: A Qualitative Study on Self-Hosters’ Motivation, Operation, and Security Mindset* (Gröber et al., 2023), a large survey of Nextcloud users (n=994) showed that privacy and autonomy are central motivating factors for self-hosting.  [oai_citation:0‡USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/grober?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Example

A blogger rents a virtual private server (VPS) where they install a content management system for their website. WordPress, Ghost, or Drupal.

A university or research lab installs and operates its own instance of Nextcloud on-premises, handling configuration, maintenance, updates, backups, and data storage locally, rather than subscribing to a vendor-hosted cloud version.
